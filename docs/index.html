Blueprint

How knowledge is held, governed, and exchanged

A brain is a locally owned, policy governed, append only audited folder of plain text. Blueprint specifies the structure and governance of one brain; its six companions specify what the brain is for, who may decide, how knowledge moves between brains, how a model may process it, what tooling leaves behind, and what an agent that learns is allowed to keep.

documents

Seven documents, one brain

One brain, three boundaries, one authority model, one reason. Knowledge crosses to another brain under SPEAK, to a model under CONFIDE, and into the tooling under TRACE. Every crossing is an act, DEFER says who may authorize it, and POLARIS says whether the brain will do it at all.

principles

Two rules that carry the design

Precedence is asymmetric, and the asymmetry is the point. POLARIS holds the highest precedence to forbid an act and no precedence to permit one. Purpose may narrow what a brain will do. It may never widen it, satisfy another document's check, or excuse a failure.

There are no sub-brains. Containment and boundary are mutually exclusive: if you can read it at will, the agent has a folder; if you cannot, it is not inside you. Whoever holds the signing key owns the refusal, and a brain you can sign for is a folder with aspirations. What looks like a hierarchy of brains is a set of peers under asymmetric agreements plus an authority graph that DEFER already governs.
