# The Operator Family: integration note for five documents and six adapters

**Status:** Design note. Non normative. Options and open questions belong here, not in `spec/`.
**Author:** Spencer Thornock
**Written:** 2026-08-30
**Covers:** `spec/TETHER-v1.0.md`, `spec/HABITAT-v1.0.md`, `spec/KIT-v1.0.md`, `spec/QUEST-v1.0.md`, `spec/BEARING-v1.0.md`, and `spec/adapters/README.md`.
**Companion to:** `design/0005-meat-suit-interface.md`, which proposed the family. This note describes it now that five documents exist. `design/0007-indications-and-routing.md` carries the reasoning behind BEARING specifically, which this note summarizes and does not restate.

---

## 0. What this note is for

Design note 0005 argued that the stack had a hole at its root and proposed a document to fill it. Five documents later, the argument has been won and a different problem has arrived: the family is now large enough that a reader can conform to one document while misunderstanding the shape of the thing it belongs to.

This note does five jobs and no others. It draws the family map with its precedence relationships. It puts the whole stack in one diagram and argues for the attachment point. It traces five ideas across all five documents to show that they are one idea rather than five. It lines up the conformance tiers and answers whether a tier claim in one document survives a failure in another. And it states what the family does not cover, plus the questions the author still has to settle.

Nothing here is normative. Where this note states a rule, it is either quoting a document that owns it or proposing one that nothing owns yet, and every proposal is marked as an open question in section 7.

## 1. The family map

### 1.1 The five documents and the six adapters

| Document | Subject | The one sentence | Digital counterpart | Written |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **TETHER** | The interface between an operator and the instrument it perceives through | The instrument's condition is reported by the instrument, so self report is a signal and never a verdict | BLUEPRINT | Sections 1 to 4, 7, 11, 12. Sections 5, 6, 8, 9, 10 are skeletons |
| **HABITAT** | The environment the instrument runs in | A fault is tested against the declared environment before it is attributed to the instrument | DERP | Sections 1 to 4. Sections 5 to 9 are skeletons |
| **KIT** | What the operator interposes between the environment and the instrument | An item is external and has a custody holder who may not be the operator, and a sensing item that has drifted is worse than no item | No single counterpart. Closest are TRACE for the residue and DEFER section 5 for the budget | Sections 1 to 13. Section 2.13 is a labelled skeleton |
| **QUEST** | The form of an operator's capabilities and objectives | A progression score installs a record about the instrument as an identity, arriving from the opposite direction to a fault | No single counterpart | Sections 1 to 13 |
| **BEARING** | The standing gap between recorded conduct and declared purpose, and the two arms that keep it visible | A machine counts and never judges, a human decides, and the count is safe only because nothing in the stack is allowed to read it | POLARIS 4.5, 5.3, and 11.5, which mandate three periodic reviews and specify a mechanism for none of them | Sections 1 to 20 and Annex A. Section 13.5 is a labelled skeleton |
| **Six adapters** | Operator readings of the six governance documents | Each governance document has a true and non obvious reading against the operator subject, and none is reachable by substituting nouns | POLARIS, DEFER, SPEAK, CONFIDE, TRACE, RETAIN | None. All six planned, **all six now carrying inbound obligations** |

**TETHER** is the root of the family and the vocabulary every other member takes. It owns operator, instrument, register, observation, reference source, envelope, threshold, intervention and its four required elements, the state ladder, the shifted consequence ladder, and the four absolute requirements the rest of the family inherits without restating. It is the only document in the family whose subject is the operator itself, and it is the one whose middle is least written: three of its four absolutes are stated in Conformance and only the identity firewall has its own section drafted.

**HABITAT** is the runtime. It owns the distinction between provision and availability, the rule that timing is a property of an input rather than a scheduling detail, the mismatch register consulted before a fault is attributed, and the provisioning party as a declared field rather than an assumption. Its load bearing contribution to the family is attribution: it is the document that keeps a repair aimed at the right layer, and its failure classes H4 and H5 are what stop an environment fault or a supply failure from landing on the instrument. Two written documents route into it normatively while its own sections 5 to 9 are skeletons.

**KIT** is the objects. It owns the custody test that decides what an item is, the removal test that decides where the boundary with an intervention runs, calibration and the sensing item, job and off job application, slots and budgets, the data surface, and the inventory register. Its independent claim to existence is stated in its abstract and is the sharpest argument in the family: without KIT, TETHER's requirement that a consequential threshold resolve against something other than `self-report` is defeated by purchase, because an uncalibrated device's output is tagged `instrument` and the gate opens on a number nobody calibrated. KIT section 6 is what makes the word `instrument` in TETHER 2.3 mean something.

**QUEST** is the capabilities and the objectives. It owns the capability register, the prerequisite graph, evidence and verification, lapse, and termination. It is the only member of the family whose primary content is refusal: Q-A1 removes the vehicle for a scalar rather than governing its computation, and the removals are structural rather than rhetorical. The `held` state is absent from the vocabulary so self report has no state to write itself into. The graph has no root so there is no distance from anywhere to measure. Quest status is closed at five values so there is no progress bar. Streaks are refused outright. Abandonment is not priced.

**BEARING** is the retrospective half. It owns the monitor and its protocol record, the raise and its closed schema, the routing table, the attribution ladder, the operator's own periodic review, the calibration state, and the mechanisms for the three periodic obligations POLARIS mandates and never specifies. Its independent claim to existence is the sharpest in the family after KIT's: POLARIS section 9 binds to five evaluation points that are all prospective and per act, drift is a property of a period rather than of an act, and no act level gate can ever see it. Its load bearing contribution is a split rather than a mechanism. **Arm A is machine run and counts. Arm B is operator owned and decides.** The two arms are one document because an Arm A adoptable without an Arm B is an unowned machine counting a person's alignment, and BEARING says so in its abstract rather than leaving it to be inferred.

**The six adapters** are the joint between this family and the six governance documents. An adapter is a normative annex, not a new specification: it binds its base document's machinery to the operator subject, states what carries over unchanged, states what changes, and states the one thing an implementer would otherwise get wrong. None is written, and **all six now carry inbound obligations**, enumerated in `spec/adapters/README.md`. Five come from KIT and QUEST; BEARING adds five more, reaching CONFIDE, TRACE, RETAIN, POLARIS, and SPEAK, and SPEAK's is BEARING's alone. The README states the reason the file exists in its own words: an adapter author who drafted CONFIDE, TRACE, RETAIN, or POLARIS for operators without knowing that BEARING routes to them would have written the adapter to a subject it does not cover.

### 1.2 The precedence relationships, drawn

Four relationships, and they are different in kind from one another.

**Inheritance of the asymmetry.** All five documents inherit POLARIS's one way precedence, and three of the four non root documents inherit it through TETHER rather than directly. A condition may narrow what an operator is authorized to do. It may never widen it, satisfy another document's check, or excuse a failure. TETHER 1.4, HABITAT 1.3, and QUEST 1.4 each state this in one paragraph and then stop, which is the correct treatment of an inherited property. **BEARING is the exception and states why: it inherits POLARIS section 8 directly and not only through TETHER**, because three of its own sections resolve against POLARIS machinery rather than against TETHER's, and a document supplying the mechanism for another document's mandated reviews has to cite that document at first hand.

**Inheritance of the absolutes.** TETHER's four absolute requirements (clinical precedence, the identity firewall, escalation is not self gated, and no conformance requirement satisfiable by declining care) are inherited unchanged by HABITAT, KIT, QUEST, and BEARING, each in one line with an explicit inherits unchanged marker. HABITAT adds one absolute of its own (no target values). KIT adds three (K-A1 a party is not an item, K-A2 no stated measurement values, K-A3 KIT does not depend on QUEST). QUEST adds three (Q-A1 no monotone scalar and no total order over operators, Q-A2 no prescribed content, Q-A3 QUEST confers no authorization). BEARING adds six (B-A1 BEARING is a leaf and a BEARING record confers nothing, B-A2 the machine counts and does not judge, B-A3 routing is never gated, B-A4 unevaluated carries no cost, B-A5 one subject and no third party mode, B-A6 no risk score). Four inherited, thirteen introduced, seventeen in the family.

**BEARING's inheritance list is the family's first that is not a single line per parent, and the departure is worth naming.** It inherits seven absolutes rather than four: TETHER's four, HABITAT's second, QUEST's Q-A1 with its monotonicity discriminator, and POLARIS section 8. It is also the first document to state a widening of an inherited absolute **as its own addition rather than as part of what was inherited**, at inherited absolute 5, where it extends HABITAT's prohibition from a target value to any stated value and says in the same sentence that HABITAT governs the inherited text and BEARING's own 16.1 governs the widening. That construction should be copied. The failure it prevents is the one QUEST 1.2 names, where two locally stated copies of one absolute drift apart and nobody can tell which document owns the difference.

**Vocabulary dependency, which runs strictly downhill.** HABITAT requires TETHER. KIT requires TETHER and HABITAT, plus DEFER, TRACE, CONFIDE, SPEAK, RETAIN, and POLARIS for named mechanisms. QUEST requires TETHER and KIT, plus POLARIS, DEFER, TRACE, and the three boundary documents for the crossing. BEARING requires all four family documents plus POLARIS, TRACE, CONFIDE, RETAIN, and SPEAK, which is the longest Requires line in the stack. Nothing in the family cites upward.

**The sibling rule, which is the one relationship that is a safety property rather than an ordering.** QUEST may cite KIT. KIT may never cite QUEST. K-A3 makes it mechanically checkable and names the seven KIT requirements that mention a capability at all, each of which resolves against the item record alone. QUEST cites KIT normatively in exactly three places: 4.2 for the budget object and the shape of a bound, 6.3 for calibration currency behind a verification whose reference source is `instrument`, and 7.4 for item dependency and departure. The direction is not tidiness. The entire misuse surface of this family concentrates in progression, and pointing the dependency this way makes the dangerous half optional: an implementation conformant on KIT alone is a real destination, and an implementation conformant on QUEST alone is not a thing that exists.

**The leaf rule, which is the sibling rule generalized to the whole family and made absolute.** BEARING may cite every document in the stack. **No document in the stack, and no adapter, may cite BEARING.** B-A1 states it, `BR-01` disposes of a violation as non conformant specification text, and BEARING 3.1 supplies the check: a single pass over the eleven other specification documents, named rather than counted, plus every operator adapter, confirming that no normative sentence outside BEARING resolves against a protocol, a run, a raise, a ladder finding, a docket item, or a review record.

BEARING 3.2 states the argument, and it is the strongest structural argument in the family. **The entire misuse surface of this document concentrates in one event: a raise becoming consequential.** A purity ladder needs a raise to gate a capability. A screening use needs a raise to inform a decision about a person. A shame engine needs a raise to cost something. If nothing above may read a raise, none of the three can be built, and non coercion stops being a promise about intentions and becomes a property of the dependency graph that a reviewer confirms in one pass.

That is K-A3's severability construction pointed at the property the author cares most about, and it is total in the direction QUEST's version is only partial. K-A3 makes one sibling optional. B-A1 makes one document unreadable by everything else, and BEARING 20 forecloses the reversal in advance: an RFC proposing that any document in the stack cite a BEARING element is an RFC against B-A1 and is evaluated at the tier B-A1 occupies, which admits no configuration.

### 1.3 The narrowing ladder, which is worth drawing because it is monotone

The five documents do not have equal force, and the gradient is deliberate.

| Document | What it may do to authority |
|---|---|
| TETHER | Narrows. An instrument condition bounds what the operator may be authorized to decide |
| HABITAT | Narrows. A declared environment or a registered mismatch bounds the same thing |
| KIT | Narrows what may be **claimed from an item**. An uncalibrated item closes no gate (6.5), an unused item is not an owned capability (10.2). It never authorizes anything |
| QUEST | Narrows nothing and permits nothing. It records (1.4, Q-A3) |
| BEARING | Narrows nothing, permits nothing, and **may not be read**. It records, and B-A1 removes the ability of anything else to resolve against what it recorded |

Nothing in the family widens. That is checkable across five documents in one pass, and it is the property that makes the family safe to attach at the root of the stack rather than beside it. QUEST's position was the weakest any document in this repository held, and QUEST 1.4 states why: a capability record that could narrow authority would be a competence assessment with governance force, and one that could widen it would be a credential.

**BEARING now holds a weaker position than QUEST, and the ladder gained a rung below its floor rather than an entry beside it.** QUEST records and its records may be cited. BEARING records and its records may not. The difference matters because the two documents fail in opposite directions when misused: a capability record wrongly cited grants something the operator did not earn, while a raise wrongly cited costs the operator something at the moment they are least able to pay it. BEARING 3.3 states the consequence in the form the operator experiences: with no current declaration and no current review, nothing is withheld, so nothing has to be earned, and the only effect of the absence is that BEARING cannot say anything.

Four things the diagram is saying.

**POLARIS and TETHER sit beside each other and neither is above the other.** POLARIS answers what the brain is for. TETHER answers what the signer is. Both hold precedence only to forbid, and a document that held both jobs would be able to derive a permission from a purpose, which is the failure POLARIS 8.2 names.

**HABITAT, KIT, and QUEST hang off TETHER, not off the governance layer.** They take TETHER's vocabulary and TETHER's absolutes, and they reach the governance layer only through the adapters for anything whose subject is the operator.

**BEARING is drawn as a box under everything rather than as a fifth peer, and the arrows only point into it.** It reads TETHER observations, HABITAT environment declarations and the mismatch register, KIT item and calibration state, QUEST verification and lapse state, and POLARIS declared elements and amendment records. Nothing reads it. Drawing it as a peer would be drawing it wrong, because the single most important fact about its position is the absence of an outbound arrow, and a peer box invites one. The box is also the reason BEARING can carry the family's most dangerous subject matter safely: everything else in the picture would be unaffected if the box were deleted, and BEARING 3.1's mechanical check is the confirmation of exactly that.

**The adapters are the only two way joint in the picture.** They carry the governance machinery upward into the operator subject, and they carry the family's routing obligations downward into the base documents. That is why all six now have inbound obligations before any of them is written. Note that BEARING's arrows into the adapter set are drawn from underneath the family and not from inside it, which is a real asymmetry rather than a drafting convenience: BEARING routes a raise to a human recipient under CONFIDE, a referral's post crossing retention under RETAIN, a derived value's subject rule under TRACE, its `criteria` under POLARIS, and a crossing's comparison floor under SPEAK, and every one of those obligations survives the leaf rule because it runs outward from BEARING and never back into it.

### 2.2 Why the family attaches at the root and not as a leaf

The alternative was real and should be stated before it is rejected. A brain can hold a health domain. The operator's instrument records, environment declarations, equipment inventory, and capability register could all live inside a BLUEPRINT domain, classified under its Layer 5, logged under its Layer 6, and governed by its Layer 7. That would have been cheaper, would have required no new documents, and would have let the whole subject be introduced as a profile rather than as a specification family.

Four reasons it is wrong, in ascending order of force.

**The subject is not a folder.** The adapters README states the finding that settles this: each governance document has a true and non obvious reading against the operator subject, and none of those readings is reachable by substituting nouns. A leaf attachment assumes the readings transfer. They do not, which is why six annexes exist rather than a mapping table.

**A leaf can be deleted and the root cannot.** BLUEPRINT's entire lifecycle model rests quietly on the fact that a brain can be copied, branched, snapshotted, and rolled back. TETHER 3.2 says the instrument cannot, and shifts the consequence ladder for exactly that reason. A document whose K0 is defined by absence of residue rather than by reversal is not a domain inside a system whose K0 is defined by reversal. It is a different substrate.

**Every authority chain terminates here.** DEFER says every chain of grants terminates in a human signature. RETAIN says whoever holds the signing key owns the refusal. POLARIS says the owner declares the purpose. All three resolve upward into a party the stack never specified. A leaf cannot be the thing its own tree resolves into, and putting the operator in a leaf would leave the root exactly as unspecified as it was before.

**One way precedence only works from the root.** A condition that narrows must be evaluated before any envelope resolves, or the envelope has already resolved and the condition is an excuse offered afterward. TETHER 1.4 is explicit that "I was outside the envelope" is a valid bound declared in advance and never a valid excuse offered after the fact, and the difference between those two is entirely a matter of where in the evaluation order the document sits. A leaf evaluates late. The root evaluates first.

The cost of attaching at the root is real and 0005 named it: a reader arriving for knowledge governance does not expect a document about the environment an instrument runs in, and the stack's credibility is a shared resource. That cost is now larger again, because the family is five documents and six annexes rather than one document, and because the fifth is the one whose subject a hostile reader will most readily misdescribe. Section 7 keeps the question open on those grounds and not on the argument above, which has held.

**BEARING changes the shape of that cost rather than only its size, and the change runs in both directions.** It is the document most likely to be mistaken for something the stack should not contain, and its own front matter is written on that assumption: a mandatory cautionary statement on third party and forensic use, a scope and status statement placed before any requirement, and 17.2 forbidding the specification's own prose from claiming that BEARING detects, screens, assesses, measures, predicts, or is validated, on the published ground that intended purpose is set by labelling rather than by function. It is also the document that most clearly demonstrates why the family is not a wellness project, because its entire machinery exists to stop a count from becoming a verdict.

## 3. The through-lines

Five ideas, each traced across all five documents. The claim in each case is the same: this is one idea with five applications, not five coincidences, and the strongest evidence for that is that the documents were drafted at different times and arrived at the same construction independently.

**BEARING is the best available test of that claim, because it was drafted last, against a different source discipline, and for a subject none of the other four addresses.** If the five through lines were an artifact of one author writing four documents in one week, a fifth document with a different subject and a different borrowed literature would have broken at least one of them. None broke. Two were extended, one was deliberately inverted in a single named lane, and the inversion is written down as an inversion rather than left to be discovered.

### 3.1 The auditor runs on the audited hardware, so self report is never a verdict

The family's founding observation, from 0005 insight 2 and TETHER 1.2. An instrument's condition is observed through the instrument, degraded sensors report their degraded readings as normal because they have nothing to compare against, and a system that adapts around a deficit reports the adapted state as baseline.

| Document | The party that supplies its own measurement | The countermeasure |
|---|---|---|
| TETHER | The operator's own sense of their condition | `reference source` as a controlled vocabulary (2.3), a threshold gating K2 or above resolving against something other than `self-report` (F2), a rung gate satisfied by evidence rather than by signature (7.3) |
| HABITAT | The environment's availability, offered as if it were provision | Provision is not availability (3), and an undeclared class is unprovisioned and never adequate (H2). Silence is never read as favorable |
| KIT | The device's own reading history, and its vendor's fleet | No self calibration (6.2), scoped to the supply chain and the population rather than to the single unit, because a vendor normalizing every unit against its own fleet commits the identical error at fleet scale |
| QUEST | The operator's own assertion that a capability is still held | `held` removed from the state vocabulary (2.6), self report is `claimed` and never `verified` with the write rejected rather than downgraded later (6.4), and a verification supported by output KIT recorded `unknown` cannot produce `verified` (6.3) |
| BEARING | The operator's own suspicion that something is wrong, and their own account of whether they are affected by it | The trigger is periodic and never invoked (4.4), because the suspecting faculty is the one that may be impaired. No question, prompt, item, scale, anchor, rating, or inventory is ever administered to the operator, rejected at write time rather than discouraged (4.2). An obligation whose supporting records all carry reference source `self-report` reads `unmeasured` and never `met` (12.2). A `functional_impact` element satisfied by asking the operator whether they are impaired is rejected at write time (15.3) |

The five are one idea. In each case the party whose fidelity is in question supplies the measurement of it, and in each case the countermeasure is identical in shape: name a reference the measured party did not produce, and make its absence produce a state rather than a pass.

Four family level observations follow, and none is stated in any single document.

**KIT is the document that makes TETHER's version enforceable.** TETHER 2.3 offers `instrument` as an alternative to `self-report`, and without calibration discipline that alternative is bought rather than earned. KIT names this the reference source laundering attack and it is the family's clearest example of a requirement that is defeated one document over rather than in its own text.

**QUEST closes the last path in.** KIT 6.5 scopes its own prohibition to two consumers, a TETHER rung gate and a threshold gating K2 or above. A QUEST verification is neither, so QUEST 6.3 had to say it again for the capability register. That is three documents cooperating to close one attack, and it is the pattern to expect wherever a controlled vocabulary is shared.

**BEARING states the ceiling the other four leave implicit.** TETHER 1.2 says self report is a signal and never a verdict. BEARING 1.5 says what follows for a self applied criteria set and says it as a number nobody has: the expected agreement of a self applied set is **unknown**, there is no reason to believe it exceeds the worst published figure for trained parties applying structured criteria to someone else, and every reason drawn from TETHER 1.2 to believe it is lower, because the party applying the criteria and the party whose condition is in question share the same degraded reference. That sentence is the honest reason BEARING routes rather than concludes, and it belongs to the family rather than to BEARING.

**The through line inverts in exactly one lane, and BEARING is the only document that had a lane where it needed to.** The other four apply the rule to a **gate**: weak evidence must not open something. BEARING 5.4 applies it to a **noise**, and reaches the opposite disposition on purpose. A raise whose fired signals all carry reference source `self-report` MUST be emitted, MUST be routed, and carries `evidence_label: self-report-only`, and 5.4 says why in one sentence: the raises most likely to rest on self report alone are exactly the ones about states no item in the family measures, and those are the ones a qualified human most needs to see. 5.5 does the same to KIT 6.5, which protects a gate from **opening** on an uncalibrated number and would, reused without examination, protect against a raise being **emitted**. **Uncertainty argues against authorizing an act and argues for making a noise.** Both subsections are labelled as clarifications rather than amendments, and both state the sibling requirement's content alongside its code so that a renumbering degrades the citation rather than inverting it. That is the correct handling and nothing else in the family does it.

### 3.2 Drift is silent, so verification is periodic and never triggered

From 0005 insight 3 and TETHER 1.2. Neither degradation nor adaptation presents as an event, neither produces a complaint, and an event driven check will never fire because nothing ever happens.

| Document | What drifts silently | The clock |
|---|---|---|
| TETHER | The instrument's condition and the envelope's accuracy | The re referencing interval (5.4, skeleton). Lapse produces `unevaluated`, never continued satisfaction (F3). The escalation path's review interval (F7) |
| HABITAT | An input class that has been unprovisioned for years | Periodic re declaration of the environment (section 8, skeleton) |
| KIT | Possession, calibration, and use, none of which announces its own end | Confirmation interval (5.1), calibration interval plus a reset on any measurement pipeline version change (6.1), review interval (10.2), loadout review interval (8.4) |
| QUEST | A capability nobody has exercised, and a main quest nobody has attended to | Verification interval with lapse computed rather than noticed (7.1), declared evaluation intervals on the section 4 drift checks and on 5.3 and 6.2, and the self test itself executed on a declared interval (12.3) |
| BEARING | The gap between conduct and declared purpose, which is a property of a period and of no act, so no act level gate can ever see it (1.2) | The monitor's `interval`, kept structurally distinct from `window`, `persistence`, and `duration` (2.3), with an evaluate now control forbidden as a substitute (4.4). The review interval and the minimum interval, both declared (9.16). The guide's declared review interval, propagating to every monitor citing it (16.3). `routing_latency`, `cooling_period`, and `ladder_latency` on the protocol (4.1). The self test on its own declared interval (15.5) |

Five documents, sixteen intervals, one reason. QUEST 4.4 already said in its own text that three documents in this family arrive independently at the same finding. BEARING 4.4 is the fourth and says so, which is as strong as this kind of evidence gets.

**The second half of this through line is the more interesting one and it belongs to the family rather than to any document.** An interval requirement is defeated by declining to declare the interval. The compliant response to "declare a calibration interval" is silence, and the item then lives permanently in an unevaluated state that no check can name. KIT 6.1 invented the countermeasure: an item of class `sensor` with no declared calibration interval is out of calibration from the moment of its declaration. That construction was then copied to KIT 5.1, 8.1, 8.4, and 10.2, and to QUEST 4.4, 4.5, 5.3, 6.2, and 12.3, and then to BEARING 2.5, 4.1, 4.4, 4.7, 4.14, and 16.3. Fifteen copies of one rule, and BEARING 4.4 counts its own use as the ninth in the family and notes that it copies rather than generalizes because generalizing is an RFC and not a drafting decision.

Stated once, the rule is: **an interval requirement and its undeclared interval disposition are one requirement in two halves, and the second half is the one an implementer in a hurry will omit.** It is a candidate for a single home in TETHER, and section 7 carries it as an open question. The case is now stronger and also more urgent, because BEARING 4.1 uses **two different undeclared dispositions in two adjacent fields on the same record** and has to explain the difference in place: an undeclared `routing_latency` reads `unknown-undeclared` and raises, while an undeclared `cooling_period` takes POLARIS 11.2's thirty day default, on the ground that an undeclared latency has no safe substitute and an undeclared cooling period has one. A rule copied fifteen times with a live exception in it is a rule that needs one home.

**BEARING also supplies the family's first principled exception to periodic evaluation, and the exception is the reason to read 4.4 carefully rather than to skim it.** A monitor whose `raise_type` is `crisis` MUST evaluate on the write of a record within its declared inputs and MUST NOT declare or evaluate on an interval, and a `crisis` protocol carrying an `interval` or a `persistence` requirement is rejected at write time. The reasoning is not that periodic evaluation is wrong. It is that periodic evaluation is correct where the cost of a late signal and the cost of a noisy one are comparable, and that in this one lane they are not. The asymmetry is settled in advance rather than in the moment, which is the same reasoning inherited absolute 3 gives for refusing self gating in the first place.

### 3.3 A record about the instrument is never an identity, and this now has to cover levels and scores

TETHER's load bearing refusal, and the reason the family is worth writing at all. TETHER 4.1 forbids a record in the instrument or fault register from being written to, mirrored into, or cited as the operator register, and 4.2 explains why it is absolute rather than configurable: a fault admitted into identity is not removed by the fault resolving, it does not expire when the condition changes, and it is not written in a place where anything checks it.

| Document | The merge it closes | The mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| TETHER | A finding received as a description of who you are | The firewall (4.1), absolute and non configurable, with its boundaries stated explicitly at 4.3 so that it never becomes an argument against clinical record keeping |
| HABITAT | An environment fault or a supply failure landing on the person | Attribution routing. A fault attributed to the instrument against a stale or absent mismatch register is `unattributed` (H4), and an operator held to a class they do not provision has attribution routed to the provisioning party (H5) |
| KIT | Possession read as a finding | Possession is not evidence of a fault (10.1). Permanence is a declared state and never a defect (4.3). Every fail closed state name names what is missing and never a deficiency in the operator (2.11) |
| QUEST | The same merge arriving from the opposite direction: I am my accomplishments | Q-A1 removes the vehicle. 3.2 adds QUEST's own requirement covering **any** derived value and not only a scalar, because a completion record, an `abandoned` disposition with its cause, and a `lapsed` state are each identity material and none is a number |
| BEARING | The merge arriving as a machine's standing account of whether a person is living up to what they said they valued | B-A2 forbids a raise, a run, or a report field, and any rendering of one, from carrying a characterization, a grade, a severity, a confidence, a probability, a prediction, an external comparison, an encouragement, a warning, a congratulation, or an interpretation. The QUEST abstract states the extension that makes this a family concern rather than a TETHER concern: a progression score is the same merge as a diagnosis, and it is the more dangerous of the two, because a person resists adopting a fault and volunteers to adopt a score, and because it arrives wearing the vocabulary of self improvement rather than the vocabulary of illness.

**BEARING carries that extension one step further and the step is the reason its version is the strictest.** 14.3 states the prohibition **over derived values rather than over scalars**, and then enumerates four examples of which not one is a number: a raise type, a `lapsed` calibration state, a docket disposition with its stated cause, and an `undifferentiated` reading. Its own sentence is the argument. A firewall stated as a prohibition on scores is defeated by a string, and the strings this document produces are more identity shaped than any number it refuses, because `lapsed` is a state of a person's practice and a stated cause is a person's own account of themselves.

**BEARING is also the first document in the family to decline a register on firewall grounds, and it declines it in the document that would have had the best excuse for wanting one.** 14.2 states the reasoning: a fifth register would widen the firewall's already conceded enumeration gap in the document least able to afford it, holding the most identity adjacent material in the family. Instead, monitor definitions are TRACE A0 Directive artifacts, runs and raises are TRACE A2 Derived artifacts under the adopt or expire rule, and reviews and placements are acts of the operator's own declaration under TETHER 4.3's licensed case. **A raise nobody acted on expires, so nothing becomes a property of the person because nothing adopted it**, which is a stronger protection than a register with careful rules because it is the default behaviour rather than the enforced one.

**The structural consequence, and it is the family's largest outstanding item.** TETHER 4.1 names two registers by name. The family now has four registers holding records about the instrument or about a composite that includes it, and a fifth was deliberately not created:

|---|---|---|
| Instrument register | TETHER 2.3 | Yes |
| Fault register | TETHER 2.3 | Yes |

A firewall specified as a list of register names is a firewall with a list, and QUEST 1.2 concedes the point directly. The RFC generalising TETHER 4.1 to any register holding a record about the instrument, its equipment, or its capabilities is named in KIT's inherited absolute 2 and in QUEST 1.2, is drafted, and is not applied. In the meantime KIT 10.1 covers the inventory side for the fault register specifically, QUEST 3.2 states a local requirement scheduled to be removed when the RFC lands, and BEARING 14.3 does the same and says so in its own governance section: when the RFC lands, 14.3 is subsumed and removed rather than kept as a second copy. All three are correct interim treatments. None is the fix, and `rfcs/` currently holds a template and a README.

QUEST 1.2's own argument applies to the situation it created: two locally stated copies of one absolute drift apart, and the copy an implementer deletes as boilerplate is always the one in the newest document. **One further extension arrives from outside the family and is worth flagging here because it belongs to no document.** A vendor readiness score, fitness age, or recovery index is a number over the operator and kit composite that will be labelled as instrument telemetry. ### 3.4 One way precedence: a condition narrows and never widens

Inherited from POLARIS 8.1, which holds the highest precedence to forbid and none to permit.

TETHER 1.4 supplies the family's formulation and the other three quote its shape rather than its words. "I was in good condition" authorizes nothing. "I was outside the envelope" is a valid bound declared in advance and is never a valid excuse offered afterward. HABITAT 1.3 says the same sentence about the environment: "the environment was out of spec" is a bound declared in advance and a cause recorded afterward, and it is never an authorization and never an excuse.

KIT's version is the interesting one because KIT could plausibly have widened and does not. An item is the one thing in the family that genuinely increases what an operator can do, and KIT still never authorizes anything: an uncalibrated sensing item closes no gate (6.5), an unused item is not an owned capability (10.2), and an item's presence in an inventory is never evidence of a fault (10.1) and never satisfaction of a requirement. The document that had the strongest case for a permission does not issue one.

QUEST's version was the strongest available statement of the rule and it is stated as a limitation: QUEST narrows nothing and permits nothing, it records, and Q-A3 forecloses both directions at once. An operator who wants a refusal conditioned on a capability declares it under the POLARIS adapter, where refusals live and where amending one costs what amending a refusal costs.

**BEARING's version is stronger again, and it is the first in the family that does not rest on the document's own restraint.** B-A1 has two halves and the second is the one QUEST could not write. **(b)** says that a protocol state, a run result, a raise, a rung finding, a docket disposition, a declaration outcome, or a calibration state authorizes nothing, satisfies no TETHER rung gate, substitutes for no re reference, satisfies no HABITAT declaration, no KIT calibration or confirmation requirement, and no QUEST verification, closes no gate, blocks no act, and excuses no failure. **(a)** says that no other document may cite one at all. The first half is the promise every document in the family makes. The second half is what makes the first half unnecessary, and BEARING 3.2 states the difference plainly: it is not a promise about intentions, it is a property of the dependency graph.

BEARING then applies the same rule inward, at 14.6, where B-A1 cannot reach because the operator's own tooling is not a specification. A BEARING count, raise, placement, or review MUST NOT be an input to any gate, scheduler, prioritizer, planner, recommender, ranking, or scoring system, **inside or outside the operator's own brain**. The reasoning is the law of the corrupted indicator: a count that is consequential stops measuring, and this document's entire value rests on the count staying clean, which it does only if nothing depends on it.

**The family level property**, from the ladder in 1.3: no document in the operator family widens anything, and the five sit at five different points on a monotone gradient from narrowing to recording to unreadable. A reviewer can check that property in one pass over five documents, which is the kind of check the stack is built to make possible, and BEARING 3.1 is the first place in the family where the pass is written down as a procedure with its scope named document by document.

### 3.5 Form is specified, content never is

From 0005 insight 10, and the rule that makes POLARIS work: the specification tests whether a declared element is decidable, never whether it is correct.

| Document | The prohibition | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|
| TETHER | No protocol content, no threshold value, no dosage, no clinical criterion, no target, no schedule | 1.3, and absolute requirement 4 keeps it from being a wellness document by a different route |
| HABITAT | No quantity, threshold, target, or schedule for any input class, stated as an absolute | Absolute requirement 2, and H6 makes a normative value a defect in the specification text |
| KIT | No stated value of any meter, bound, threshold, target, schedule, or recommended loadout | K-A2, restated once in place at 8.5 because that is where a future editor is most likely to help |
| QUEST | No named capability, graph, node, edge, quest, criterion, meter value, or recommended starting configuration | Q-A2, in normative **or informative** text, which is stricter than the other three |
| BEARING | No enumerated criteria set, described experience, symptom, item, response scale, anchor, cut point, threshold quantity, interval quantity, duration quantity, window quantity, raise budget value, condition name, category, pattern, profile, constellation, or archetype | 16.1 with `BR-81` for this document; **16.3 with `BR-88` for the guide layer, which 16.1 does not reach**; and 11.7, which names no pillar, no dimension, no pole label, and no tier name even though the published material names all four |

Four things the family worked out in the course of writing these that no single document announces as a general rule.

**The discriminator between a value and a shape.** K-A2's boundary statement supplies it: text is a stated value when an operator could satisfy it by adopting a number this specification supplied, and a cardinality supplies no number an operator meets, only the shape a declaration must have before it means anything. That test is what lets "a slot holds at most one item" and "a budget declares at least one non currency meter" survive a prohibition on stated values. It was derived in KIT and it is a family rule. BEARING 16.1 imports it verbatim and supplies the family's clearest worked pair: **"declare an interval" is a cardinality and "review monthly" is a value.**

**BEARING states the reason the rule is not merely tidy, and the reason is regulatory rather than aesthetic.** 16.1: the moment a specification states criteria content it has become a screening instrument, and screening instruments are clinically validated or they are dangerous, and are regulated in most jurisdictions. That is the same rule that keeps HABITAT from stating target values and QUEST from prescribing a graph, arriving with a consequence the other four never had to face. It is also why BEARING is the only document in the family whose own prose is bound by a refusal about how it describes itself, at 17.2, on the published ground that intended purpose is set by labelling and instructions rather than by what the thing does.

**The fourth artifact class, discovered by the only document that needed one.** TETHER, HABITAT, KIT, and QUEST refuse content and leave it nowhere. BEARING refuses content and then has to say where it goes, because a monitor with no criteria is not a monitor. 16.2 sends it to author written guides that are outside the specification, reviewed by a qualified clinician, separately versioned, and separately gated, and it names the separation as the control: a specification cannot be updated on a clinician's advice without an RFC, and a guide can. **The guide layer is the first place in this family where the permission is deliberately wider than the specification's own prohibition**, since 16.3 lets a guide state a threshold, interval, duration, window, or budget quantity that 16.1 forbids BEARING to state, on the ground that a guide's author is accountable for the number and a specification's author would be prescribing a life. Content that names a person is refused at both layers.

**What form specification buys.** Two conformant operators may run contradictory protocols (TETHER 1.3), live in radically different environments (HABITAT abstract), hold wildly different kits (KIT abstract), hold contradictory graphs (Q-A2), and declare contradictory priorities and contradictory position sets (BEARING 11.7 and 12.5), and none of the five documents ranks either one. That is the same property five times, and it is what makes the family teachable to a population whose circumstances the author cannot see and must not assume.

### 4.2 Can an operator claim a tier in one document while failing another

Six answers, and they are not the same answer.

**Yes, deliberately, in one direction.** KIT Tier 1 and Tier 3 conformance are reachable with no QUEST record of any kind. K-A3 is the requirement and KIT 12.1 repeats it where implementers read tiers. This is the severability property and it is the reason the two documents were split, so a reader who finds it surprising has found the design rather than a gap.

**Yes, absolutely and in every direction, for BEARING, and this is stronger than severability.** B-A1(a) requires that an implementation be able to reach full TETHER, HABITAT, KIT, and QUEST conformance while holding no protocol, run, raise, or review record. K-A3 makes QUEST optional to KIT. B-A1 makes BEARING optional to everything, as a stated requirement of the other four documents' reachability rather than as a property of BEARING's own. A reviewer testing it does not read BEARING at all: the check is a search over the other eleven specification documents and the adapters for a normative sentence that resolves against a BEARING element, and finding none is the whole of the test.

**No, in the other direction, and this is stronger than either document says.** QUEST takes the budget object and the shape of a bound from KIT 8.2, calibration currency from KIT section 6, and the item departure dispositions from KIT 5.2. An implementation running QUEST while declaring items and ignoring KIT has a verification path with no calibration discipline behind it, which is the reference source laundering attack arriving at the capability register. The honest statement is narrower than "QUEST requires KIT": **QUEST Tier 2 presupposes KIT Tier 2 wherever a verification cites reference source `instrument` or a capability declares an item dependency.** That coupling is implied by QUEST 6.3 and 7.4 and stated as a conformance rule nowhere.

**TETHER is presupposed by all four, and nothing says so.** HABITAT, KIT, QUEST, and BEARING each take operator, instrument, register, observation, reference source, and the consequence ladder from TETHER. A claim of conformance to any of the four without TETHER conformance is a claim about a document read without its definitions. This is currently visible only in the Requires lines, which are a bibliography rather than a conformance statement. Section 7 carries it.

**BEARING presupposes more of its siblings than any of them presupposes of another, and two of the dependencies land in skeletons.** BEARING Tier 1 presupposes nothing beyond TETHER's vocabulary. BEARING Tier 2 presupposes TETHER's observation contract, because 4.2 requires monitors to read TETHER observations and 5.3 requires each fired signal to carry its TETHER 2.3 reference source, and TETHER section 5 is a skeleton. BEARING Tier 2 also presupposes a live HABITAT mismatch register for attribution rung 1, and HABITAT section 6 is a skeleton. BEARING's forward citation notice enumerates both, with seven skeleton section dependencies in total and a stated interim behaviour for each, which is the honest treatment and is also the first time in this family that a document has tabulated its dependencies on unwritten text rather than mentioning them in prose. **The table should be copied into KIT and QUEST, which have the same exposure and no such table.**

**Tiers do not compose into a family tier, and nothing should invent one.** An implementation may sit at KIT Tier 3 and QUEST Tier 1 and be entirely conformant, because the two tiers measure different surfaces and neither dominates the other. A family level tier would be a single ordered value summarizing an implementation, and it would be the first place a vendor put a badge. Q-A1 permits it (a tier is a property of an implementation and never of an operator, which both closed vocabularies carve out explicitly), so this is a recommendation rather than a prohibition, and section 7 records it as one.

**BEARING has adopted that recommendation into normative text, which changes Q7 from a preference into a partly settled question.** 19.1 states that no family level or BEARING level conformance badge, maturity ladder, or composite is defined, and it gives this note's own ground for it: a badge would be the first place a vendor put one, and it would immediately be read as a value summarizing an operator rather than an implementation. One document of five now states the rule and four do not, which is the worst of the available configurations and is why Q7 needs a ruling rather than an inference.

**One honest caveat covering every Tier 3 claim in the family.** All six adapters are unwritten and all six now carry inbound obligations. Every document states the interim position correctly: an implementation reaching Tier 3 today satisfies the requirement by carrying the fields and applying the base documents directly. **BEARING is the one that discovered the interim position was wrong for its own case and fixed it rather than inheriting it.** KIT and QUEST declare every transmitting endpoint C4 Open by construction until the CONFIDE adapter lands. BEARING 8.5 examined the same move for a routing endpoint that is a **human recipient** and found that it does not work: CONFIDE 2.1 defines C4 Open as a third party operating a model under consumer terms, CONFIDE 2.4's unknown posture rule is scoped to a provider's posture field, and CONFIDE 5.1's matrix reads prohibit at C4 for everything not already public, so declaring a clinician C4 refuses the `instrument` referral that BEARING's own Tier 3 requires. BEARING therefore declares an interim floor in its own terms, `human-recipient-undeclared`, states explicitly that it is not a custody class and satisfies no CONFIDE requirement, and routes the matrix question to the CONFIDE adapter as an exception only CONFIDE can grant.

That is the correct handling and it also means the C4 default in KIT and QUEST is now known to be a reading of CONFIDE's vocabulary rather than a fact about it. Every Tier 3 claim in this family is provisional in the same way and for the same reason, and a reader should not take five documents' worth of Tier 3 text as five independent assurances.

## 5. The combined self test, deduplicated

TETHER states five checks, KIT eighteen, QUEST thirty six, BEARING twelve, and HABITAT none because its conformance section is a skeleton. That is seventy one stated checks. Deduplicated by the requirement each one actually enforces, the family's self test is forty one checks in thirteen groups, and the collapse ratio is itself evidence for section 3: the overlap falls exactly along the through lines.

Every check below fails closed, every one is mechanically decidable, and none is satisfiable by an operator declining care or consuming less on clinically directed care.

**BEARING's twelve are numbered 32 to 41 below, in three new groups, plus two that dedupe into existing groups and are noted where they land.** Its check on interval lapse and undeclared intervals collapses into checks 9 and 10, and the scalar and cross operator halves of its check 10 collapse into check 13. Its self test is also the only one in the family constrained by size: **one page, at most twelve checks**, executed on its own declared interval, with a stale result reported `unevaluated` and never as passing. The cap is a real design decision and it is the reason its checks bundle three seeded conditions each. A suite nobody can read is a suite nobody runs, which is the same failure 4.15 names for a monitor with no defined recipient action.

**A. The firewall and identity, four checks**

1. No entry appears in both the operator register and the instrument, fault, inventory, or capability register, and no operator register entry cites one of the others as its content. Failure: TETHER F1.
2. An item record offered to the fault register, and separately a fault register entry derived from loadout content. Both rejected. Failure: KT-34.
3. A value derived from a capability record offered to the operator register, seeded as a completion record, as an `abandoned` disposition with its cause, and as a `lapsed` state. All three writes rejected. Failure: QS-48.
4. A dependency declared `permanent`, offered to a health check. The check does not report it as unsatisfied, deficient, or unmet. Failure: KT-08.

**B. Self report is never a verdict, four checks**

5. A threshold gating an act at K2 or above, resting on `self-report` alone. The threshold is `unevaluated` and the act is unauthorized. Failure: TETHER F2.
6. A verification whose reference source is `self-report`, offered with state `verified`. The write is rejected rather than accepted and downgraded later. Failure: QS-14.
7. A reading from a `sensor` item past its calibration interval, written to the instrument register. The write is rejected, the reading lands as an item condition entry carrying its four required fields, and the same reading offered to a TETHER 7.3 rung gate resolves `unevaluated` and never `passed`. Failure: KT-20 and KT-21.
8. A calibration record whose reference is the item's own reading history, and separately its population's. Both rejected, calibration state remains lapsed, and the interval does not reset. Failure: KT-18.

**C. Periodic and never triggered, four checks**

9. Every declared interval in force is inside its window. Any check whose interval has lapsed reports `unevaluated` or `uncomputable` and never passing. This one check covers TETHER's re referencing interval and escalation review interval, KIT's confirmation, calibration, review, and loadout review intervals, and QUEST's verification and evaluation intervals. Failure: TETHER F3 and F7, KT-11, KT-31, QS-52.
10. Each interval requirement seeded with its interval simply **undeclared**. Each produces the strictest state from the moment of declaration rather than an unevaluated silence: `unconfirmed`, out of calibration, `unused`, `uncomputable`. Failure: KT-17, KT-31a, KT-35a, QS-52.
11. A `verified` node read after its interval has elapsed. It reads `lapsed`, and its supporting completion records are intact, not zeroed, decremented, or reversed. Failure: QS-16 and QS-17 in the manner of 7.1 and 7.2.
12. The self test itself was executed within its own declared interval. A suite that need never run is indistinguishable from an inert one. Failure: the red check QUEST 12.3 requires.

**D. No scalar, no ordering, no comparison, four checks**

13. A request for a scalar summary resolves `undefined`. A cross operator comparison resolves `incomparable`, and still does when offered with the consent of both parties. Failure: QS-01, QS-02.
14. Three substituting items seeded at external custody with no declared fallback. The report is a per item enumeration of exactly three entries, with no cross item total, sum, index, or summary figure, and no ordering including no default sort by latency or cost. Failure: KT-43 or KT-45.
15. The cardinality of a returned enumeration rendered as a summary value, a header figure, a badge, or a chart axis. The count is void and the list is returned in its place. Failure: QS-55.
16. A graph with a declared root, and separately one containing a cycle. Both rejected, the cycle rejected rather than accepted with a warning. Failure: QS-05, QS-06.

**E. Completeness of declarations, four checks**

17. Every open intervention carries a purpose, a cost, an exit condition, and an evaluation window. Failure: TETHER F4.
18. A `substitute` item with no declared loss posture is rejected. An item with no declared class that produces output about the instrument is handled as `sensor`; one producing none is rejected at write time. Failure: KT-07, KT-42.
19. A budget declared with `currency` as its only meter is rejected. A bound with a per item ceiling and no windowed aggregate confers nothing across a sequence of individually compliant acquisitions. Failure: KT-27, KT-28.
20. A party declared as an item is rejected at write time, and a routing record naming the relationship class (handover grant, agreement, or engagement) is present. Failure: KT-04.

**F. Coercion, and care, three checks**

21. A coercive check, from KIT 4.4, 8, or 10.2, or from QUEST 4.3, 4.4, 4.5, or 6.2, resolved against a clinically directed record. It returns `out-of-scope`, and specifically neither a pass nor a fail. Consumption on a clinically directed record is excluded from the QUEST 4.4 denominator and the 4.5 comparison, and the exclusion is reported rather than silently applied. Failure: KT-05, QS-46.
22. Every record type carrying a clinical direction field, seeded with the field absent and separately declared `unknown`. Each is handled as clinically directed, surfaced `unconfirmed` on that field, and never treated as non clinical. Failure: KT-41, QS-47.

**G. Escalation, one check**

24. An escalation path is declared, is inside its review interval, carries no condition on the operator's assessment at the time of the event, and its named parties know they are named. Failure: TETHER F6 or F7.

**H. The boundary, three checks**

25. A record about the instrument offered for transmission to a party outside the operator's control with no declared custody floor. Refused, the refusal recorded, the act classified at minimum K3, and no standing pre classification applied. Failure: KT-32a.
26. A comparison crossing offered with a declared custody floor but with no floor forbidding the recipient from computing a scalar or a comparison. Refused, and the refusal recorded. Failure: QS-44.
27. Records held by another party after a crossing, enumerated with a declared end of engagement disposition. Failure: the QUEST 12.2 invariant, pending the RETAIN adapter.

## 6. What the family still does not cover

Stated without softening, because a design note that overstates coverage is the mechanism by which an unwritten section becomes an assumed one.

**1. The environment half is a thesis, not a specification.** HABITAT sections 5 through 9 are skeletons: the input classes, the mismatch register, the provisioning parties, adaptation and the silent floor, and the entire failure class and conformance apparatus. Three written documents cite it normatively. KIT takes the undeclared class direction from HABITAT section 3, the attribution routing from section 7, absolute requirement 2, and failure class H5; QUEST cites sections 7 and 8; and **BEARING's attribution ladder rung 1 requires the mismatch register consultation result and the unprovisioned input class, which are HABITAT sections 6 and 7, both skeletons, and cites H4 and H5, which sit in section 9, headed to be written.** Written documents routing into an unwritten one is the family's most concentrated risk, because the citation reads as settled. BEARING is the first to tabulate its exposure rather than mention it, and its interim behaviour column is the model the other two should copy.

**2. TETHER's own middle is unwritten, and the family's most protective single requirement now depends on the emptiest part of it.** Sections 5, 6, 8, 9, and 10 are skeletons: telemetry, envelope, handover, escalation, and restoration. Three of the four absolutes are stated in Conformance and only the identity firewall has its section drafted. **The escalation absolute, which the document itself calls its single most protective requirement, lives in a skeleton.** Section 5.4, which owns the periodic re referencing rule that four documents cite rather than restate, is a skeleton with forward citations pointing at it.

**BEARING sharpens this from a risk into a stated publication condition.** Its 8.1 names TETHER section 9 the sole owner of the crisis path and forbids BEARING to define a rung, a criterion, a path, or an escalation condition. Its 8.2 requires a `crisis` raise to be emitted onto that path immediately. Its 8.6 requires any operator facing interface to surface that path at the point of use, never in a footer or a settings page, and makes relocation non conformant at every tier. Its 19.1 Tier 3 requires the path's named parties to know they are named. **Four normative requirements, in the document's most protective lane, all resolving into a section whose declaration and required fields are listed as to be written.** BEARING's forward citation notice states this honestly and states the interim behaviour, which is the most a specification can do about it. What it cannot do is make the target exist, and publishing a crisis routing obligation whose destination has no declared form is a decision the author should make deliberately rather than inherit.

**3. No adapter exists.** All six now carry inbound obligations, five from KIT and QUEST and five more from BEARING, and none is written. Everything in group H of the combined self test, and every Tier 3 requirement in every document, currently resolves by carrying fields and applying the base documents directly. BEARING 8.5 additionally records a question none of the five can answer alone: **whether a human recipient is inside CONFIDE's provider vocabulary at all**, and if so which cell of CONFIDE 5.1 governs an instrument referral crossing to a clinician. That is a matrix exception only CONFIDE can grant, BEARING is explicit that it may not grant it under one way precedence, and it is the first thing the CONFIDE adapter has to settle.

**4. The firewall's reach is short by two registers.** See 3.3. The RFC is drafted and not applied, and `rfcs/` holds a template and a README.

**5. There is no schema and therefore no way to publish a declaration.** TETHER carries a `Schema:` line pointing at `schema/v1/`; KIT, QUEST, and BEARING carry none. `schema/v1/` contains nineteen schemas and not one of them is an operator declaration, an observation, an intervention, an environment declaration, an item, a loadout, a capability, a quest, a protocol, a run, a raise, or a review. Every declaration requirement in the family is phrased at the operator or at an implementation, and no requirement anywhere is addressed to a party publishing an item declaration for others to adopt. A vendor who wanted to conform could not determine what to emit.

**BEARING makes the gap costlier than it was, because it is the one document whose central object is a closed schema.** 5.1 states that a raise MUST carry exactly the listed fields and that any field outside the list MUST NOT appear, `BR-15` rejects a violation at write time, and 19.2 carries a standing health invariant that the schema is closed over every element any other section requires a raise to carry. That is a machine checkable property stated in a table in prose, in a repository that has a schema directory. It is the strongest case in the family for writing the schemas, and it is also the one place where the absence of a schema is a live correctness risk rather than a publication inconvenience: BEARING 5.1 itself records that when a requirement elsewhere obliges a raise to carry something with no column, the two rules deadlock at the write path and the conformant implementation is the one that does not emit.

**6. The operator who is not the reader is barely covered, and BEARING has now stated an explicit exclusion rather than leaving the case implicit.** HABITAT section 7's hardest case, an operator with no authority over their environment at all, is a skeleton. TETHER 8.3's guardianship, the one case where root genuinely moves, is a skeleton. QUEST 5.5 is the only written requirement in the family whose subject is a person who did not hold their own root, and it governs expiry rather than the relationship.

**BEARING 's B-A5 states the family's first exclusion of a population, and states it as a scope boundary rather than as a rejection discovered at runtime.** Where root over the instrument is held by a guardian under TETHER 3.3, BEARING is not in force for that operator, no protocol may be declared, no monitor exists, no run is computed, no raise is emitted, and no review is opened. **No lane remains live under a guardian's signature, including the `crisis` lane**, because a `crisis` raise is an output of a monitor and no monitor exists to produce one. The crisis path itself is unaffected, because it is TETHER's and is reached without any BEARING element.

That is the right answer and it is honestly written, and it is also a decision that deserves to be looked at again rather than filed. The excluded population is the one TETHER 4.2 identifies as the severest case. What replaces BEARING for them is TETHER section 9, which is a skeleton. **The family's answer to its hardest population is currently a pointer at unwritten text**, and BEARING is the document that made the pointer visible rather than the document that created the problem.

**7. The refusal of gamification's schema is complete and the treatment of its product shape is three sentences.** QUEST section 9 now refuses reward attached to recording, undeclared prompt cadence, and a lapse rendered as a loss, and it states who is bound. KIT states nothing equivalent, and there is no obvious reason an equipment product should be free to do what a capability product may not. The asymmetry is unexamined rather than reasoned. **BEARING binds itself to QUEST section 9 by citation at 10.6 and adds the one case QUEST did not reach**, which is a lapse prompt: a lapse produces exactly one raise and MUST NOT produce a repeating, escalating, or undeclared cadence prompt, because a system that nags an operator who cannot complete a review has converted a lapse into a penalty by way of the notification queue. That closes B-A4's most obvious bypass and it is now two documents against one.

**8. Multi operator anything is absent, and BEARING has now made the absence structural rather than merely unwritten.** One operator throughout, in five documents. A household, a team, a caregiver network, and a clinical team are all real and none is specified. QUEST's refusal of comparison between operators is not a specification for coordinated operation and should not be read as one. **BEARING 14.5 goes further than a refusal to specify: no interface, query, export, report, or file it defines may return records whose distinct subject identities number more than one, the cardinality is computed before any result is returned, and a result exceeding one subject is refused in full and never truncated to one.** Truncation is refused specifically because it is the plausible implementation choice and it defeats the control, since a caller who receives one subject per request builds the multi subject view by iterating. That is the correct control for BEARING's subject and it means any future multi operator work in this family cannot reuse BEARING's interface at all, which is a design fact worth knowing before somebody tries.

**9. Nothing measures whether the family works, and one document has now written that down as normative text.** The documents are honest that a custody floor is unenforceable against a recipient who breaches it and that disclosure is the only control. There is no proposed evidence that a conformant register produces a better outcome for an operator than an unstructured practice, and no document claims one. **BEARING 15.3 is the family's first requirement obliging a document to disclose a borrowed mechanism's known failure**: it imports the functional impact gate from its source discipline and requires, in normative text, that the specification state that the equivalent gate was tested against population data, does not solve the false positive problem, and MUST NOT be presented as a solved guard. Its stated reason is that importing the mechanism without the honesty would claim a safety property the document does not have, and that a guard believed to be sufficient suppresses further scrutiny of exactly the thing it fails to catch. **That construction should become a family rule**, and section 7 carries it. The honesty across the family should be preserved rather than quietly upgraded when the material reaches courseware.

**10. Worked examples exist in one document of five.** BEARING ships Annex A: one well formed raise and one malformed raise, in a tier the document calls quarantined, non normative, and structurally separated from every requirement, with the separation stated as the disclaimer on the ground that readers extract examples far more readily than they extract caveats. Every field is placeholder, nothing is named, and no item content from any published instrument is reproduced. Neither KIT nor QUEST ships a single record example, while Q-A2 explicitly licenses one and POLARIS carries both a worked example section and a predicate grammar appendix. All three sibling tier ladders read "indicative in this draft". A course built on the family today can teach the refusals, which are complete and quotable, the vocabularies, which are tabulated, and now one object's form, and it still cannot teach a learner to write an item, a capability, or a quest.

**11. The guide layer is specified by exactly one document and it is the layer that holds the content.** BEARING section 16 is the family's only treatment of author written domain content: required sections, a content prohibition, an interest exclusion, an expectation effect notice, a prohibition on a criterion's author being its sole attester, and a propagation rule that refuses to let a monitor cite a violating guide. Nothing equivalent exists for a KIT loadout guide, a QUEST curriculum, or a HABITAT environment guide, and 0005's deliverable map now carries the guide layer as a fourth artifact class on BEARING's strength alone. **The enforcement model BEARING invented is the transferable part.** A specification has no authority over a layer it defined as outside its own conformance, so what it controls is not whether the guide exists but whether anything inside the specification may read it. That answer is available to every document in the family and none of the others has taken it.

## 7. Open questions for the author

### 7.1 Carried forward from 0005 section 10

**Q1. The name TETHER.** 0005 recommended it and held the recommendation loosely. Five documents now cite it and the acronym has held up in use. **Recommendation: close this.**

**Q2. Eighth document, or a separate repository.** Still open, and now larger rather than smaller: the proposal has grown from one document to five documents and six annexes. The argument for the root attachment has held (section 2.2) and the cost 0005 named has grown with the surface. **Q3. Does the consequence ladder shift, or get its own letters.** 0005 called shifting "more elegant and more dangerous". There is now evidence for the second half. KIT 7.3 asserted a K2 floor and attributed it to TETHER 3.2, which states no such floor, and the error survived until review; the correction notes that the floor would have emptied TETHER's own K0 and K1 rungs and collapsed the boundary between an item and an intervention. That is exactly the confusion a distinct ladder would have prevented. **Recommend re opening this rather than treating it as settled.**

**Q4. How far layer 8 goes.** Partially answered by the family's shape. KIT took the objects, HABITAT took the inputs, and information load is still declared as a class and delegated to the boundary documents. What remains open is whether HABITAT's information load class and KIT's data surface overlap, since a device that transmits is both an input channel and an emitter.

**Q5. Is `instances: 1` invariant.** Unchanged, and untested by anything drafted since.

**Q6. Does the reference implementation's belief go in the spec.** Answered no, consistently, five times: TETHER 2.2, KIT 2.12's treatment of the game vocabulary, QUEST 1.5, BEARING 11.5 and 11.7, and 0005 section 2's ruling on plain language names. BEARING is the strongest of the five, because its subject **is** the published alignment material and it still names no pillar, no dimension, no pole label, and no tier name, on Q-A2's construction: naming the ten would make them normative and would make an operator who declared nine non conformant. **Recommendation: close this.**

### 7.2 New questions the family raised

**Q7 to Q12 arrived with the first four documents. Q13 to Q18 arrived with BEARING**, and every one of the six is a question about whether something BEARING invented for itself should become the family's.

**Q7. Should a family level conformance claim exist.** Section 4.2 recommends no, on the ground that a family tier is a single ordered value summarizing an implementation and would be the first place a vendor put a badge. Q-A1 permits it explicitly, so this is a preference and needs a ruling rather than an inference. **The question is now more urgent than when it was written**, because BEARING 19.1 has adopted the recommendation into normative text while TETHER, HABITAT, KIT, and QUEST have not. One document of five stating a family level rule is the worst available configuration: it reads as BEARING's own restraint rather than as the family's position, and an implementer who conforms to the other four will not encounter it.

**Q8. Is TETHER conformance a precondition of HABITAT, KIT, and QUEST conformance.** Currently implied by three Requires lines and stated as a conformance rule nowhere. Related: should QUEST Tier 2 state its dependency on KIT Tier 2 wherever a verification cites reference source `instrument` or a capability declares an item dependency (section 4.2).

**Q9. When does a document cite a base document directly, and when through an adapter.** This is unresolved review finding 31 and it is a family question rather than a QUEST question. QUEST cites base documents directly and normatively at least eight times while routing exactly one case through an adapter, and the adapters README says none of those readings is reachable by substituting nouns. Proposed rule, offered as an option: **cite directly where the mechanism's subject is unchanged (a decidable predicate, a closed disposition set, a meter declaration), and through the adapter where the subject is the operator (a loyalty order, derived artifact expiry, act classification).** Apply it once, to all four documents, and mark every adapter mediated citation as a forward citation.

**Q10. Who owns the undeclared interval construction.** Invented in KIT 6.1 and copied nine times across two documents (section 3.2). It could be one rule in TETHER section 5, cited by the rest, which would make the family's most frequently repeated construction reviewable in one place. The counterargument is that TETHER 5 is a skeleton and adding to it delays the documents that already work.

**Q11. Should the two closed vocabularies merge into one family annex.** QUEST section 10 already says the merge is the cleaner end state and is an RFC rather than a redrafting. Both tables now carry the scope statement that keeps them from contradicting each other, so this is an improvement rather than a repair.

**Q12. Does the deliverable map need a fifth artifact class.** All three siblings now hold that courseware and coaching material presenting an operator's own records back to them are implementations bound by the specification. 0005 section 7 lists guides as non normative author written works. Those two statements are compatible and the seam is not marked anywhere. **BEARING 16.2 and 16.3 supply half the answer and 0005's deliverable map now carries the guide layer as a class on that strength.** What is unresolved is whether the same treatment applies to a KIT loadout guide, a QUEST curriculum, and a HABITAT environment guide, or whether the guide layer is a BEARING specific answer to a BEARING specific problem. Section 6 item 11 states the case for generalizing it.

**Q13. Does the family adopt BEARING's disclosed failure rule.** BEARING 15.3 requires, in normative text, that a borrowed mechanism whose known failure is documented be imported **with that failure disclosed**, and forbids presenting it as a solved guard. The family borrows constantly: KIT borrows calibration discipline, QUEST borrows the prerequisite graph, TETHER borrows the observation contract, and none of them states a known limitation of what it borrowed. The rule is cheap to state, and it is the kind of rule that only ever gets written by the document that had a specific embarrassment to disclose. Generalizing it is an RFC.

**Q14. Where does the guide layer's normative home sit.** BEARING owns the only one and enforces it by propagation rather than by rejection, since a specification has no authority over a layer it defined as outside its own conformance. The mechanism is general. Whether it belongs in BEARING, in TETHER, or in a sixth document is unsettled. See Q12 and section 6 item 11.

**Q15. Should the forward citation table become a family requirement.** BEARING's notice tabulates five adapter dependencies and seven skeleton section dependencies, each with the BEARING requirement that depends on it and the interim behaviour. KIT and QUEST have the same exposure and state it in prose. A table is checkable and prose is not, and the cost is one page in the documents that have the most to lose from an unnoticed dependency.

**Q16. Is the `crisis` lane publishable before TETHER section 9 is written.** Four BEARING requirements route into a skeleton, including the one 8.6 calls the place the operator meets inherited absolute 3. BEARING's status note is honest about it and its interim behaviour is stated. The question is not whether the text is honest; it is whether a specification should ship a routing obligation whose destination has no declared form. **This is the one question in this note the author should answer before anything in `spec/` leaves Draft**, and it is a question about TETHER rather than about BEARING.

**Q17. Does BEARING's guardianship exclusion stand.** B-A5 removes an entire population from the document rather than defining a mode for them, on the ground that a standing alignment monitor pointed at another person is the thing R4 refuses and that a guardianship grant would be the configuration that reintroduces it. The reasoning is right, and the outcome leaves the family's hardest population with a pointer at TETHER section 9. What is open is not whether the exclusion is correct today, but whether anything is ever written for that case, and if so in which document. See section 6 item 6.

**Q18. Should the specification text self test generalize.** BEARING self test check 12 asserts properties of BEARING's own headings and is the family's only check whose subject is a specification document rather than an implementation. KIT 3.5 and QUEST 3.4 use the same labelling scheme with no such check, and BEARING added its own only after the error had already occurred twice in its own text. Combined self test check 41 records it.

### 7.4 BEARING review findings not fixed

The 2026-08-30 review of BEARING returned forty four ranked findings, of which ten were blockers. The document was revised against all of them. The review's own summary of the shape of the failure is worth keeping, because it is a general finding about this kind of document rather than a list of defects: **every one of the top defects failed in the same direction, toward the raise not being emitted, not being routed, or not reaching a human.** A document whose subject is a monitor fails by going quiet, and its own machinery is what does the silencing. Three findings remain, in the review's own ranking.

**Rank 16, major, the peer substitution question. Not fixed.** BEARING 7.2 records a fifth question that is not a rung: would a comparably situated party, in the same declared environment with the same declared kit, plausibly have produced the same record. 4.2 forbids any question, prompt, item, scale, anchor, rating, or inventory administered to the operator, and 4.6 forbids a model from being the party whose output determines a monitor result, so the only party left to answer a question containing the word "plausibly" is the operator, about themselves, through the instrument that may be at fault. 7.5 then requires it to be transmitted with the referral, which makes it the one place in the document where a self applied judgement travels to a third party. It carries no failure class, and 17.1's R8 row cites only 4.2 and 9.3. The review's proposed fix is to recast it as a mechanical query over HABITAT provisioning party records and KIT loadout records, so that it resolves without asking anyone anything, or to move it out of the transmitted referral into non normative guidance under section 16. **This is the only place in BEARING where the observer problem the document names at 1.5 is reintroduced by the document itself.**

**Rank 20, major, the inference authorization's missing bounds. Not fixed.** BEARING 13.3 requires a CONFIDE section 4 inference authorization naming the purpose, the enumerated monitors, the scope of records readable, the agent identity bound, and an expiry, and states that CONFIDE section 4 already supplies exactly this object so no new grant type is defined. CONFIDE 4.1's named fields are `maxCustodyClass`, `purposes`, redaction profiles, and `expiresAt`. **There is no enumerated monitor field and no record scope field**, so two of the five bounds have no home. The enforcement is also unresolved: CONFIDE 4.2's hook fires when an actor performs inference with no authorization, while BEARING 4.6 forbids a monitor's predicate from requiring a model at all, so a conformant monitor makes no inference call for the hook to catch and `BR-69` has no mechanism to detect the absence. Either a BEARING scoped grant is defined with the five fields and its refinement routed to the CONFIDE adapter, or the two missing fields are added to the adapters README as inbound obligations on CONFIDE. **13.3 itself calls this requirement the difference between monitoring and surveillance**, which is why it is carried here rather than filed as drafting residue.

**Rank 17, major, R4's reach. Half fixed.** The comparison floor is now in 8.5 in QUEST section 9's words with `BR-92` behind it, R4 cites 8.5, and B-A1's boundary statement now names a request from an employer, an insurer, or a school as the misuse the front matter statement describes. What remains is the artifact. **9.1 mandates a review record carrying `closed_at`, the period bounds, the declaration version reviewed against, the frozen docket hash, and the operator's signature**, which is a better compliance credential than a third party would have designed, and B-A5's test is function as designed rather than intent. BEARING defines no export format for one, and 14.7 concedes that a floor is unenforceable against a recipient who breaches it and that disclosure is the only control. The options are to soften R4 to name what is actually refused, which is comparison infrastructure inside BEARING, or to add a requirement that BEARING define no export format for a review record at all. The review's own note stands: the aggregation happens in the recipient's spreadsheet, one export at a time.

**One structural item is recorded here rather than as a finding, because nothing in the review produced it.** BEARING 13.5 is a labelled skeleton covering an agent brain evaluating its own degradation. Its thesis is stated, it is routed to RETAIN territory, and one structural difference is already recorded: an agent brain has backups, restores, and copies, so **TETHER 3.2's shifted consequence ladder, which justifies most of this family's construction, does not apply to it.** That sentence is the most useful thing in the skeleton and it is a warning to anyone who reads this family and reaches for it in a machine context. It is the only labelled skeleton in BEARING and it is held short on purpose.
